\section{Covariance and correlation of linearly dependent variables}

Let \(X\) be a real-valued random variable with \(\E{X}=0\)
and finite second moment. For \(Y=aX+b\), with constants \(a,b\in\R\),
we derive \(\Cov{X}{Y}\) and find the correlation for
positive and negative \(a\).

\subsection*{Covariance}

First, \(\E{Y}=a\E{X}+b=b\), so centering \(Y\) gives
\(Y-\E{Y}=aX\). Hence
\begin{align*}
  \Cov{X}{Y}
  &= \E{(X-\E{X})(Y-\E{Y})} \\
  &= \E{(X-0)((aX+b)-b)} \\
  &= \E{X\cdot aX} \\
  &= a\E{X^2} \\
  &= \boxed{a\Var{X}},
     \qquad \bigl(\E{X}=0\bigr).
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Correlation coefficient}

To obtain the standard deviation of \(Y\), we use the same centered form.
\begin{align*}
  \Var{Y}
  &= \E{(Y-\E{Y})^2} \\
  &= \E{(aX)^2} \\
  &= a^2\Var{X}.
\end{align*}
Writing \(\sigma_X=\sqrt{\Var{X}}\) and
\(\sigma_Y=\sqrt{\Var{Y}}\) gives
\(\sigma_Y=\abs{a}\sigma_X\). For \(a\ne 0\) and \(\sigma_X>0\),
\begin{align*}
  \rho_{X,Y}
  &= \frac{\Cov{X}{Y}}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} \\
  &= \frac{a\sigma_X^2}{|a|\sigma_X^2} \\
  &= \frac{a}{|a|}
   = \boxed{\begin{cases}
        -1, & a<0, \\
        +1, & a>0.
      \end{cases}}
\end{align*}
If \(a=0\) or \(\sigma_X=0\), the denominator is zero and the
correlation coefficient is undefined.
