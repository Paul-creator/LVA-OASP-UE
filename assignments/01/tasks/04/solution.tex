\section{Covariance and correlation of linearly dependent variables}

Let \(X\) be a real-valued random variable with \(\mathbb{E}[X]=0\)
and finite second moment. For \(Y=aX+b\), with constants \(a,b\in\mathbb{R}\),
we derive \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\) and find the correlation for
positive and negative \(a\).

\subsection*{Covariance}

First, \(\mathbb{E}[Y]=a\mathbb{E}[X]+b=b\), so centering \(Y\) gives
\(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y]=aX\). Hence
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)
  &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y])\bigr] \\
  &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X-0)\bigl((aX+b)-b\bigr)\bigr] \\
  &= \mathbb{E}[X\cdot aX] \\
  &= a\mathbb{E}[X^2] \\
  &= \boxed{a\operatorname{Var}(X)},
     \qquad \bigl(\mathbb{E}[X]=0\bigr).
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Correlation coefficient}

To obtain the standard deviation of \(Y\), we use the same centered form.
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Var}(Y)
  &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y])^2\bigr] \\
  &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(aX)^2\bigr] \\
  &= a^2\operatorname{Var}(X).
\end{align*}
Writing \(\sigma_X=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}\) and
\(\sigma_Y=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}\) gives
\(\sigma_Y=|a|\sigma_X\). For \(a\ne 0\) and \(\sigma_X>0\),
\begin{align*}
  \rho_{X,Y}
  &= \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} \\
  &= \frac{a\sigma_X^2}{|a|\sigma_X^2} \\
  &= \frac{a}{|a|}
   = \boxed{\begin{cases}
        -1, & a<0, \\
        +1, & a>0.
      \end{cases}}
\end{align*}
If \(a=0\) or \(\sigma_X=0\), the denominator is zero and the
correlation coefficient is undefined.
